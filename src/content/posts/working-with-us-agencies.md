Working as a subcontractor for agencies is a different job from working with direct clients. The agency owns the relationship, the brief and the deadline. Your job is to make their team look good. This mock article collects the habits that make that partnership work across borders and time zones.

## Overlap beats availability

Being online all day matters less than being reliable inside a few shared hours. Agree on a daily overlap window, protect it, and use it for decisions. Everything else can happen asynchronously.

## Write things down

Calls are good for alignment and bad for memory. After every call, send a short recap with three parts:

1. What we decided
2. What I am doing next, and by when
3. What I need from you

It takes five minutes and saves days of rework.

## Match their process, then improve it

Every agency has its own tools for tasks, files and feedback. Start by working exactly the way they do. Once you have shipped something together, suggest one improvement at a time.

## Price the scope, not the hours

Agencies sell projects to their clients, so they need predictable costs. A clear scope with a fixed price, plus a simple rate for changes, is easier for them to resell than an open hourly estimate.

> The best compliment from an agency partner is a second project, sent without a pitch.

## Handoff is part of the work

A project is not done when it launches. It is done when the agency team can maintain it without you. Leave a short handoff document, a recorded walkthrough and a clean project file.

## Be easy to recommend

Agencies refer people who make their lives simpler. Reply quickly, flag risks early, and never surprise them in front of their client.
